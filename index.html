From Circuits to Code: My Engineering Journey as Electrical & Software Systems Engineer; Shadrack W Favour

Building at the intersection of Electrical Engineering, Software Systems and Super Artificial Intelligence.

Shadrack Favour Electrical & Software Systems Engineer

There is a point in an engineer's journey when the question changes.

It is no longer simply, “How does this system work?”

It becomes:

“What can I build with what I know?”

For me, that question has shaped a journey from electrical engineering into software development, artificial intelligence and systems thinking.

As an Electrical & Software Systems Engineer, I represent a growing generation of engineers who are not choosing between hardware and software, but exploring what becomes possible when both worlds are brought together.

My story begins with Electrical Engineering.

But it does not end there yet...

The Foundation: Electrical Engineering

I earned my Bachelor's degree in Electrical Engineering from Moi University (Nairobi), establishing the technical foundation that continues to influence how I approach technology.

Electrical engineering teaches something that extends beyond circuits, power systems and electronics: systems thinking.

A component rarely exists in isolation.

A circuit depends on other circuits. A control system depends on feedback. A power system depends on reliability and coordination. An embedded system brings hardware and computation together.

The discipline therefore demands an understanding of how individual parts interact to produce a functioning whole.

That way of thinking became one of the foundations for my broader technological journey.

My engineering background includes exposure to areas such as embedded systems, power electronics and FPGA-based projects, giving me experience across different layers of modern electronic systems.

But as technology evolved, another layer increasingly captured my attention.

Software.

When Engineering Met Software

Moving into software was not about leaving electrical engineering behind.

It was about expanding the engineering toolbox.

Software offered something fundamentally different: the ability to create systems that could be rapidly developed, deployed, connected and continuously improved.

I began exploring computer programming beginning with web development and software systems, developing an understanding of how digital products are designed around users, information and processes.

The transition created a useful combination.

Electrical engineering provided the systems foundation.
Software provided the digital interface.
And together they created a broader Engineering perspective.

Rather than seeing programming as simply learning how to write code, I increasingly approached software as another medium through which engineering problems could be solved.

That distinction has become central to my professional identity.

Beyond Coding: Thinking in Systems

There is a difference between writing software and engineering a system.

Code is only one component.

A complete system requires architecture, data, interfaces, security, usability, scalability and an understanding of the environment in which the technology will operate.

This systems perspective increasingly informs my work.

My projects have explored areas including web applications, AI-powered tools, digital platforms and automation-oriented solutions.

The objective is not technology for its own sake.

It is to understand a real problem and determine whether technology can make the process more intelligent, efficient or accessible.

That mindset has also influenced the development of my personal digital portfolio View My Portfolio, where engineering, software and emerging technologies are presented as connected parts of the same professional journey.

Enter Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence represents the next significant layer of my technological exploration.

AI is powerful because it changes what software can do.

Traditional software generally follows predefined instructions.

AI introduces systems capable of identifying patterns, processing complex information and supporting decisions in ways that were previously difficult to automate.

For an engineer, this creates an exciting intersection.

Electrical systems generate data.
Software processes it.
Artificial intelligence can interpret it.
Automation can act on it.

Together, these technologies can form intelligent systems.

This is the space that I'm increasingly interested in exploring.

My work with AI applications, including experimentation with computer vision and face detection, reflects an interest in moving beyond theoretical knowledge toward working technology.

The larger objective is not simply to say that AI is the future.

It is to understand how AI can become useful infrastructure.

Technology Should Solve Something

One principle sits behind my approach to technology:

A good technological solution begins with a real problem.

Africa does not lack problems that technology could potentially address.

There are inefficiencies in business processes.
Fragmented information systems.
Manual workflows.
Infrastructure challenges.
Limited access to sophisticated digital tools.
Opportunities for automation across industries.

The challenge is not simply building another application.

It is identifying where technology can create genuine value.

This is where the combination of engineering and software becomes particularly powerful.

An engineer can examine a problem as a system.
A software developer can turn the solution into a digital product.
AI can potentially make that product more intelligent.
And entrepreneurship can take the solution beyond a prototype and toward scale.

That intersection increasingly defines my direction.

Building with Africa in Mind

My technological ambition is closely connected to the opportunities emerging across Africa.

The continent is experiencing rapid digital transformation, but many sectors still operate with systems that were never designed for today's scale of information, connectivity and automation.

That creates an enormous engineering opportunity.

The next generation of African technology does not necessarily need to copy solutions built elsewhere.

It can be designed around African realities.

Different infrastructure.
Different markets.
Different constraints.
Different user behaviour.
Different opportunities.
What can African engineers build when they stop thinking only about participating in technology and start thinking about creating it?

That question is increasingly relevant to my own ambitions.

From Engineer to Builder

Technical knowledge is only the beginning.

The larger ambition is to become a builder.

My long-term interests include artificial intelligence, automation, SaaS and technology entrepreneurship.

This means thinking beyond individual applications toward products and systems that can solve problems repeatedly and at scale.

It also means learning the disciplines surrounding technology:

Product thinking.
User experience.
Business models.
Data.
Security.
Infrastructure.
Leadership.
And ultimately, the ability to turn an idea into something people can actually use.

The transition from engineer to technology entrepreneur therefore represents another stage of the journey.

The question evolves once again.

“Can I build it?”

becomes

“Should it exist, who needs it, and how far can it go?”

The Engineer of Tomorrow

The boundaries between engineering disciplines are becoming increasingly fluid.

An engineer may work with sensors in the morning, cloud infrastructure in the afternoon and machine-learning models in the evening.

Hardware can communicate with software.

Software can interact with intelligent models.

AI can influence automated systems.

And data can connect everything together.

This emerging environment requires engineers who can understand multiple layers of a technological ecosystem.

That is the professional space that I'm deliberately building toward.

Electrical & Software Systems Engineer is more than a title.

It represents a philosophy.
Understand the physical system.
Understand the digital system.
Understand the intelligence layer.
Then understand how they work together.

Still Building

My journey is far from complete.

There is no single project that defines the destination.

Instead, each project, experiment and technical challenge becomes another opportunity to learn, build and refine the larger vision.

The ambition is ultimately not to be known simply as an electrical engineer.

Nor merely as a software developer.

Nor simply as someone interested in artificial intelligence.

It is to become an engineer capable of moving across these domains and using them together to create meaningful technology.

Technology that solves problems.
Technology that scales.
Technology that can contribute to Africa's digital future.

Engineering What Comes Next

The future will not belong exclusively to hardware engineers, software developers or AI specialists.

Increasingly, it will belong to people capable of understanding how these disciplines connect.

That is the direction I am pursuing.

From circuits to code.
From code to intelligent systems.
From individual projects to scalable solutions.
And ultimately, from engineering technology to building companies around it.

The journey is still unfolding.

“Don't just understand how systems work. Build systems that make the world work better.”
Shadrack W Favour
